How to Improve Reaction Time

Reaction time is trainable. With the right exercises most people cut 30 to 60 ms off their response time within a few weeks, and here is the routine to do it.

What is a normal reaction time?

A typical visual reaction time for adults is between 200 and 250 ms. Trained athletes reach 180 to 200 ms, and elite players occasionally dip below 160 ms. Times around 300 ms or slower are usually caused by fatigue, distractions or input lag rather than by biology.

  • 180-200 ms: trained and competitive range.
  • 200-250 ms: normal for most adults.
  • 250-300 ms: common when tired or distracted.

What makes reaction time slower?

How fast you react depends on preparation and environment more than on age. A screen with input delay, a wireless mouse without a low-latency receiver, and caffeine or sleep debt all add real milliseconds.

  • Sleep debt: adds 50-100 ms on average after a short night.
  • Display and mouse latency: adds 10-40 ms.
  • Expecting the signal: anticipation alone saves 20-50 ms.

The training routine that works

Mental and physical preparation beat random clicking. The following routine trains both the detection of a signal and the speed of the response.

  1. Test your baseline with a 30-click reaction test and write down the average.
  2. Do 10 practice runs focusing on anticipating the signal, not on spamming the button.
  3. Take a 2-minute break, then repeat 10 more runs.
  4. Log your best average per session and track improvement across a week.

Physical tips for faster reactions

Sit with your forearm at desk height, keep your hand relaxed, and position the reaction finger directly on the button. Waiting for the signal while ready to press is faster than any psychological trick.

  • Keep the finger resting on the button, not hovering.
  • Use a wired mouse or a wireless mouse with a 1000 Hz receiver.
  • Reduce display input lag by checking refresh mode and disabling energy saving.
  • React to the signal, do not try to predict the exact moment.

How to benchmark your improvement

Measure your average from 30 runs each session and compare sessions, not single clicks. A reliable test gives a mean value that changes slowly and honestly. If your average drops by 20 ms or more after a week with the routine, you are improving.

Frequently asked questions

Can reaction time really be improved?

Yes, within limits. Targeted daily training usually improves visual reaction time by 20 to 60 ms in the first weeks before the gain plateaus.

What is the average reaction time of a gamer?

Regular players average around 180 to 220 ms in click reaction tests, which is faster than the general adult average of 200 to 250 ms.

Does reaction training help in real games?

Partially. Faster clicking helps, but games also depend on prediction and game sense, so train reaction alongside map awareness rather than instead of it.
