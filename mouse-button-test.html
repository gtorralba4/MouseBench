    How to use this mouse tester

    • Click the left, right, middle and side buttons on your physical mouse and confirm the matching zone lights up blue while pressed.
    • Each zone turns green once tested. A red flash while you press means events fired that shouldn’t have.
    • Hover over the diagram and spin the scroll wheel — both arrows should light up. The scroll-distance counter tells you how much a single flick produces.
    • Watch the double-click counter: if it climbs while you’re only single-clicking, the left switch is bouncing — the classic sign of switch wear.

    Switch mouse models

    Pick 3-button, 5-button or MMO 9-button above the diagram. The diagram adapts: gaming mice add thumb buttons. Note that browsers only report two distinct side-button values (4 & 5 / back & forward) — extra thumb buttons on MMO mice share those values, so verify extra macros in your vendor software.

    Tip: testing buttons 20–30 times in quick succession is the best way to expose an intermittent or bouncing switch.

    What your results really mean

    A healthy left switch fires exactly one event per deliberate click. If the double-click counter climbs while you click slowly and carefully, the micro-switch is bouncing — the first symptom of switch wear, and it usually gets worse. One stray double-click in a few hundred is normal; repeated bouncing at slow click speed is the sign that the switch is on the way out.

    • Zones that never register while the button clearly moves usually come from settings or remapping in the vendor software, not a dead switch.
    • Browsers only expose two side-button values (back/forward). Extra thumb buttons on MMO mice share those values, so test those in your vendor software.
    • Each zone turns green after it has been tested; red flashes mean unexpected events, i.e. debounce noise.

    Compare left against right: if only the left switch bounces, it is the switch, not your system. Twenty to thirty fast presses expose intermittent bounces better than slow ones.

    Complete guide: mouse button testing

    A mouse button test watches the raw click events your mouse emits: which button fired, how many times, and whether a click arrived as one clean press or as a double. That is exactly the signal a game sees, so a button that misses or chatters here will do the same under pressure.

    Most gaming builds use mechanical switches rated for tens of millions of clicks. They wear out by contact degradation: the switch starts to bounce or stop registering, producing double-click or missed-click symptoms even when the shell looks new.

    • Double-click one press registering as two: the classic first sign of a worn or oxidised switch.
    • Missed clicks a press that never registers, common with failing mechanical switches or a weak contact spring.
    • Hot spots double-clicks that only appear on one edge of the button, typical of uneven switch wear.

    How to get the most accurate result

    1. Click each button 10–20 times at a rhythm close to your real use.
    2. Keep your finger in the same spot the whole time — edges stress a different part of the switch.
    3. Repeat the round once the mouse has warmed up; worn switches fail more visibly when warm.
    4. Test the same buttons wireless and wired to separate transport issues from switch issues.

    Why your result looks wrong

    A single double-click can be a stray event, especially in the first seconds after plugging in. Repeat the round a few times: a pattern that reproduces in several rounds is a real switch fault, while a random one-off usually is not.

    Frequently asked questions

    What does double-click detection actually test?

    It counts dblclick events. If that counter rises while you’re deliberately single-clicking slowly, the left micro-switch is bouncing from wear — the first stage of double-click failure.

    Why don’t my extra thumb buttons light up?

    Browsers only expose two side-button events (values 3 and 4, usually mapped to back/forward). Buttons beyond that can’t be individually detected by any website — use Logitech G Hub, Razer Synapse or similar to test them.

    The right-click menu keeps appearing — can I still test it?

    The context menu is suppressed inside the diagram area so right-click works normally there. Outside that area standard browser menus still apply.

    Does this send my mouse data anywhere?

    No. Everything runs locally with JavaScript; no network request is made.

    What causes double-clicking in gaming mice?

    Mainly mechanical switches. The contact spring loses tension or the contact pads oxidise over time, so the switch bounces and reports two presses. Shell tension and known first-year faults in certain models play a part too.

    Can a switch miss clicks even when it feels fine?

    Yes. A switch that has begun to fail can oscillate between clean and missed presses depending on temperature, grip and click speed. If the miss repeats across several rounds, plan a replacement or a higher debounce time.

    Can a USB hub or extension cable change my results?

    Transmission faults show up as missed or doubled clicks. Plug directly into a rear port; hubs, long extension cables and aggressive USB power saving make button events less reliable.
