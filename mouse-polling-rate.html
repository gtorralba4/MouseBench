What Is a Mouse Polling Rate?

The polling rate is how often your mouse reports its position to the computer, measured in hertz. It is one of the most misunderstood specs in gaming, so here is what it really does.

Polling rate in simple terms

Every mouse has an internal rate at which it sends data to the PC. At the standard 125 Hz the mouse reports 125 times per second, meaning one sample every 8 ms. At 1000 Hz the same mouse reports every 1 ms. The rate defines how often the cursor and click information is refreshed, not how fast the sensor moves.

The best polling rate for everyday use

For office work, browsing and casual use, 125 Hz or 500 Hz is completely fine. Your screen usually runs at 60 Hz, which is about 16.6 ms per frame. A poll every 8 ms is faster than the display can show, so you will not notice a difference in normal tasks.

What 1000 Hz really gives you in gaming

At higher rates the cursor position is sampled more often between screen frames, which reduces input lag and makes motion feel smoother on high-refresh monitors. For competitive shooters this can matter, but the gain is measured in single milliseconds.

  • At 60 Hz, doubling the rate from 500 to 1000 Hz changes almost nothing.
  • On 240 Hz or 360 Hz monitors the smaller gaps between polls become visible.
  • For click-heavy games, the click is reported on the next poll, so a higher rate records clicks slightly earlier.

How to check your current polling rate

Many gaming mice let you read the rate in their software (Logitech G HUB, Razer Synapse, SteelSeries GG). For a browser check, move the mouse over a fast USB test on a high-refresh display while watching the measured Hz, or simply trust the software reading. Wired mice usually report up to 1000 Hz; some wireless mice need a dongle or receiver to reach it.

  • Open the manufacturer software and look for the polling rate or report rate setting.
  • Set the rate to 1000 Hz if the mouse supports it and the USB port is USB 3.0 or faster.
  • Keep 125 Hz for wireless office mice to save battery; the difference in normal use is invisible.

Polling rate vs DPI: know the difference

DPI controls how far the cursor travels for a given physical movement. Polling rate controls how often the position is sampled. A high DPI with a low rate feels choppy, while a moderate DPI with 1000 Hz feels precise. Both work together, so tune DPI to your sensitivity and keep the rate at the maximum the mouse supports.

Frequently asked questions

Is 1000 Hz polling rate worth it?

For competitive gaming on high-refresh monitors, yes. For office work and casual use, the difference is invisible, so battery life and price matter more.

Does polling rate affect click speed?

Indirectly. A click is only detected on the next poll, so a higher rate can register a very fast click slightly earlier. Test your own clicking with the CPS test after changing the rate.

Can a low polling rate cause stutters?

On a 60 Hz screen, mostly not. On high-refresh screens, a 125 Hz rate produces noticeable unevenness, which is why gamers set 1000 Hz.
