    What’s a good reaction time?

    Sub-200 ms is exceptional, 200–240 ms is a good human average, 250–300 ms is typical, and above 300 ms suggests tiredness, distraction, or higher display latency. Pro esports players often sit in the 130–170 ms band for visual stimuli.

    How the test works

    After you click to start, the screen turns green after a random delay. The time between the color change and your click is measured with the browser’s high-resolution timer. The whole run is 5 rounds; averages smooth out one lucky or unlucky click.

    Clicking before green counts as an early start and that round is thrown away — patience is part of the skill.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why does the delay vary every round?

    A random delay (1–3.5 seconds) stops you from anticipating the exact frame. Anticipating can understate true reaction speed, so randomness keeps the measurement honest.

    How is the time measured so precisely?

    Both the color switch and your click carry a high-resolution performance.now() timestamp (microsecond precision before rounding), and the difference between them is your reaction time.

    Is a slow laptop going to make my time worse?

    Slightly. Total measured time includes display refresh timing and OS/javscript overhead, so comparing results on the same machine is the fairest baseline.
