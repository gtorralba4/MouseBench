How to read the results

  • Reverses — a signal that flips direction while you scroll one way. A few can be human error; several while scrolling smoothly point to a dirty encoder or loose connection.
  • ΔY total + event count — high-resolution wheels emit small fractional deltas; stepped wheels emit fixed values (often 100). A wheel that stops producing events mid-run is dropping ticks.
  • Horizontal — tilt-wheel mice report deltaX. Repeated deltaX with no intentional tilt suggests noise.
For the most reliable reading, keep scrolling in one direction for the full duration and don’t change speed abruptly. Slow, steady, full turns work best.

Common wheel faults this catches

Dead zones in one direction, bounce-back on release, ghost scrolling after the wheel stops, and crosstalk where pressing the middle button also fires a wheel event. If Reverse and jitter stay at zero over a 60s run, the encoder is healthy.

Frequently asked questions

What causes reverse scrolling?

Dirty or oxidized encoder contacts, a worn wheel assembly, or (on wireless mice) low battery. Cleaning the encoder with isopropyl alcohol fixes most cases; persistent issues mean replacing the encoder.

Why does my free-spinning wheel show huge deltas?

High-resolution wheels (Logitech free-spin, etc.) report many small fractional deltaY values per revolution. That’s normal — compare event counts within the same mouse family.

Does this work on touchpads?

Touchpads don’t produce wheel events — this test is for physical mouse wheels only.
